Gong's AI Fluency Program

AI fluency is a revenue skill. Here's how Gong builds it.

Every revenue team has access to AI. Very few have fluency: the ability of every seller, manager, and operator to put AI to work on their own deals, their own pipeline, their own Monday morning. Gong built a structured program to close that gap for its entire go-to-market organization: a five-level fluency model, a role-specific curriculum, and a custom-built learning app that makes progress visible to every leader, every week.

Why it exists

Buying tools doesn't change behavior. A program does.

Access isn't fluency

Most companies hand the team licenses and hope. Gong treats AI like any other revenue skill: taught deliberately, practiced on live work, and held to a standard. The question every course answers: "It's Monday. How do I actually use AI in my role this week?"

Buyers are asking about AI

AI now comes up in customer conversations: which tool, which workflow, what's real versus hype. A seller can't answer that credibly until they use AI fluently themselves. The program builds internal mastery first, then turns it outward.

If you can't measure it, it's a hope

Every manager at Gong can see their team's progress (completion, depth, and pace) rolled up by leader and broken down by role. AI fluency is reviewed like pipeline: weekly, not annually.

The model

A five-level fluency ladder

Every GTM employee can locate themselves on one ladder. The curriculum exists to move people up it. The levels give leaders a shared language for where the team actually stands.

LEVEL 1

The Risk

Uses unvetted tools without a security or accuracy mindset.

LEVEL 2

The Novice

Can write a prompt and use AI for simple tasks, but mostly as a passive "user."

LEVEL 3

The Practitioner

Co-creates with AI, fact-checks outputs, and iterates in the context of their actual role.

LEVEL 4

The Strategist

Orchestrates multi-step workflows across tools: chaining, verifying, and owning the result.

LEVEL 5

The Architect

Builds new systems, coaches peers, and leads AI transformation across the team.

The first goal is blunt: get everyone off Level 1. The lasting goal is a team of Strategists and Architects, people who run AI as a system, not a novelty.

The curriculum

Five courses, each one a level transition

Courses unlock sequentially. The first two build a shared foundation for the whole org; from AI 301 on, every module is tailored to the learner's role: the course spine is shared, the examples, exercises, and playbooks are theirs.

The delivery

Run inside a custom-built app, itself built with AI

The program runs in an internal learning platform (codenamed Brainstorm) that Gong's enablement team built in-house, with AI, in weeks rather than quarters. The program practices exactly what it teaches.

🎯

Role-tailored by design

Roughly 80% shared spine, 20% role-specific overlay, composed per learner, so an SDR and a sales leader study the same concepts through entirely different workflows.

✅

Knowledge checks that gate progress

Every module ends in scenario-based checks built around real judgment calls, not trivia. You pass by demonstrating fluency, not attendance.

🔓

Sequential unlocks

Courses unlock in order. Nobody jumps to orchestration before they've proven the fundamentals, one rung at a time.

🏆

Leaderboards & momentum

First-to-finish races, speed boards, and team standings turn enablement into something the field actually competes over.

📊

Manager line of sight

Every leader sees live completion rates, role breakdowns, and rollups across their org: the data to coach with, not a quarterly LMS export.

🧭

A real tool stack, taught honestly

The curriculum teaches a standard stack (Gong, Glean, Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) and one rule of thumb: keep the work where your customer data lives, and reach outside only for what that system can't do yet.

The operating framework

The 4Ds: one framework, every level

From a first prompt in AI 101 to a multi-tool chain in AI 401, every learner applies the same four disciplines.

D

Delegation

Decide which work to hand to which tool, and which steps stay with you.

D

Description

Frame the task so the tool gets exactly what it needs: nothing lossy, nothing extra.

D

Discernment

Verify every output, and at every hop of a chain, because error compounds downstream.

D

Diligence

Own the end-to-end result, internally and increasingly in front of the customer.
